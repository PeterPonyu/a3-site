Abstract

A map of where one LLR can be trusted

Same claims as the Proteins manuscript. Scope chips below keep the 69-protein plateau (0.725) separate from the 217-assay corroboration (0.702). No TeX pointers.

0.725 = 69-protein subset 0.702 = 217-assay ProteinGym

Zero-shot variant-effect scores from protein language models (PLMs) are widely used as drop-in predictors of mutational consequence, yet their reliability is reported task by task with inconsistent scoring conventions, making it hard to know where a single score can be trusted. We assemble a uniform, raw-score audit of one representative model family (ESM-2) across six decision axes — discrimination versus model scale, robustness across masked-language-model encoders, clinical pathogenic/benign transfer, protein-stability ΔΔG calibration, protein–protein binding ΔΔG, and antibody developability — using a single discipline: scores are read off the raw per-position log-likelihood ratio (LLR), never a clamped or per-assay-rescaled transform; every headline carries a bootstrap interval; and each de-circularization control is checked for applicability before its verdict is trusted.

The resulting boundary atlas is consistent and bounded. Discrimination of deep-mutational-scan fitness rises from 35M to 650M parameters and then plateaus, with 3B giving no further gain (raw-score protein-mean AUROC 0.725 at both 650M and 3B, n = 69; the 3B−650M difference is within the bootstrap interval), so 650M is the efficient operating point. The same plateau on the full ProteinGym benchmark (217 assays attempted; 201 scored) is protein-mean AUROC 0.702 — do not paste 0.725 onto the 217-assay story. The signal is robust across independently trained masked-LM encoders rather than ESM-specific: ProtBERT reaches protein-mean AUROC 0.711 and ESM-1v agrees closely.

Zero-shot LLR discriminates ClinVar pathogenic from benign variants per gene (mean AUROC 0.78 over 20 genes) under an explicit base-model-leakage caveat; the score is nonetheless calibratable on the study panel and supports selective abstention. Against ΔΔG the model is a weak fold-stability proxy, not an interface detector: binding ΔΔG correlation is carried by general fold destabilization (overall Spearman 0.20) and collapses for antibody–antigen interfaces (0.08) and for non-interface positions (≈0). For antibody developability no BH-significant associations are detected on polyreactivity (0/14 datasets) or aggregation (0/7). The uniform protocol caught and retracted three of its own earlier over-claims, which we report as evidence that the discipline, not any single ranking, is the contribution.

Plain-language significance. Protein language models can score how damaging a mutation is without being trained on the answer. We test one widely used model the same way across six jobs. The map is encouraging for ranking mutation effects and transferring to curated disease labels, and clearly bounded elsewhere: it does not understand binding interfaces and is essentially blind to the antibody-developability liabilities that matter for screening.
